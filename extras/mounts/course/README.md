# course

A course as a template, and the first composition whose networking is read off a table.

`course.grove` declares six norms. Each is one row: where a thing sits, what it is, and in its metadata who
writes it (`by`) and who reads it (`for`). The mount is that file and nothing else: it publishes the unit and
makes nothing. A world makes a course by writing one record beneath `/<self>/io/scope` that names the unit
(`make`, a reference to `/<self>/gov/course`) and where the course goes (`rel`, say `/app/course`). Its scope
foot mounts the template there, writes the table beneath it from the norms, and keeps the course. A world that
only takes part in someone else's course has the same mount and writes no such record. From then on:

- a seat that holds an invitation's number has its invitation minted, and is filled when its invitee is heard
  (`scope.foil`);
- whoever fills a seat is who the table means by it, and each world writes into the others' roots exactly what
  the table says (`weave.foil`).

- a norm that names an action (`apply`) is a place where its writer may ask for that action of the thing the
  place sits beneath. Here a seat may ask for more days on an assignment (`extend`). The chair, who writes
  assignments, applies it with the template its own world holds, and writes an answer beside the ask. No code
  crosses between worlds (`weave/answers`).

Nothing here says what an assignment or a mark is, or who may see one or ask what, except the table.

Slots are spelled by plain names (`title`, `due`, `days`), so a record reads the same in every world that
holds the template. A template's own slots are spelled under the world that mounts it, and would not.
