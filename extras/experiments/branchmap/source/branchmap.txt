Abstract
We introduceBranchmap, an EVM smart con-
tract suite implementing the notion of ‘multiverse
map’ from multiverse finance. It is a semantic names-
pace for events, conditional claims on assets, and
integration layer for defi protocols to make them
‘multiverse-aware’. We introduce a mathematical
framework for multiverse finance, putting it in the
context of Arrow-Debreu complete markets. We then
explain howBranchmaprealizes this mathematical
framework, and then explore some applications, such
as impact markets, how options are a special case
of multiverse finance, and new financial instruments
only made possible via this framework. We also dis-
cuss the mathematics of a multiverse portfolio.
Keywords:multiverse finance; prediction markets; con-
ditional tokens; namespaces;EVM; defi; event identity;
impact markets; branch cash; conditional assets.
1. Introduction
1.1 What is Branchmap?
In this paper we introduce concepts beginning to be known
as multiverse finance, as well asBranchmap, an EVM
blockchain semantic namespace designed to enable multi-
verse finance by wiring up event venues such as prediction
markets and decentralized finance (defi) protocols such as
decentralized exchanges, lending protocols, tokenized trad-
ing strategies, etc, into one substrate.Branchmapand
its associated SDKs will enable on-chain finance developers
and institutions to deploy a variety of novel financial in-
struments, including but not limited to: assets conditioned
on the outcomes of one or more events, smart contracts
whose behavior is contingent on event outcomes, lending
and borrowing contingent on events, contingent perpetual
futures, option-like multiverse “collars”, contingent predic-
tions markets, and innumerable other exotic instruments
that were previously impossible.Branchmapprovides
the plumbing that allows existing defi protocol develop-
ers to make their protocols ‘multiverse-aware’, such that
they can create alternate versions of their protocols where
users are able to interact with that protocol as though
an event outcome has already occurred. In addition, the
onchain semantic nature of theBranchmapnamespace
allows for discoverability that makes it easy for humans
and agents to determine which events have liquidity for
which assets, which protocols serve given events, and how
they are resolved, all in one place.
The core primitive ofBranchmapis a namespace of
events and possible outcomes. In general, these events are
sourced from onchain prediction markets, as prediction
markets binary outcome contracts function as “cash” in
a given universe. That is to say, given an evente and
outcome o, if you possess a binary outcome contract on
a prediction market that is exchangeable for$1when e
resolves aso, this is the same thing as having$1in the
universe whereehas resolved aso. Thus, prediction market
binary outcome contracts serve as the numeraire in a given
universe.
Each evente gets its own entry in theBranchmap
namespace. Inside of this entry is listed every crypto-
graphic asset (such as ERC-20’s and ERC-721’s), every
DEX, every lending protocol, every perpetual future, etc.
that form a coherent ‘financial universe’ that can be in-
teracted with. Thus the namespace creates discoverability
for events and every smart contract that is contingent on
outcomes of that event.
1.2 Directional vs. non-directional traders
There are two primary modes by which a multiverse finance
trader could interact with a given event: directional and
non-directional.
A user who believes thate will impact their assets and
wishes to position their portfolio ahead of time such that
they have already made the trades that they would like
to make under every possible outcome ofe is working in
thenon-directionalmode. Unlike in a prediction market,
they are not making any sort of bet as to whether a
given outcome occurs. They are depositing their assets
into theBranchmapsmart contract, receiving a copy of
that asset conditioned on every possible outcome in the
event, make trades and other financial decisions within
each possible outcome, and then when the event does
resolve and the outcome is finalized, the assets conditioned
on that particular outcome are made ‘real’ once again.
A user who believes that a given asset under a given out-
come, sayBTC ife resolves aso, is underpriced, and would
like to therefore buyBTC specifically in that universe, is
said to be working in thedirectionalmode. This type of

1.3 Why a namespace?
A namespace is valuable when it is is the caking agent
for many related objects which can be grouped by name
that need mutual discoverability. Multiverse finance begs
for this because there are numerous event venues, assets,
oracles, claims, resolution rules, token wrappers, pools,
lending markets, and composite instruments that all need
mutual compatibility with one another in order to form a
coherent system.
Another reason we created theBranchmapnamespace
is to give event venues and defi protocol operators owner-
ship over their own domains of the namespace. An event
venue, for instance, will be able to claim a top-level domain
under which they will be able to control how events are
listed and resolved, their metadata, and create additional
allowances within the namespace specific to their venue
that the default template for event venues does not provide.
A DEX operator, on the other hand, will be able to claim
a subdomain under a given event and list the contracts for
all assets available for trade on their DEX contingent on
that event.
The initial pilot program ofBranchmap, however, will
be more centrally controlled by Branchpoint Labs, the
corporate entity developingBranchmap. This is to ensure
that we can react quickly to any issues that may arise,
as decentralized processes move slowly. As we discuss
with ecosystem partners over time, we will refine what
affordances the namespace needs to have to successful
decentralize over time, leading towards the ultimate goal of
makingBranchmapinto the credibly neutral ‘namespace
for all events’.
1.4 Prior art
Here we discuss various related projects that have inspired
the design and architecture ofBranchmap.
1.4.1 Gnosis conditional token framework
TheGnosisConditionalTokenFramework[citation], widely
used by prediction markets going as far back as Omen,
and today by PM’s such as Polymarket and Limitless,
provides event-conditioned assets, and those familiar with
Gnosis CTF and the commutative settlement algebra for
assets conditioned on multiple events is replicated within
Branchmap. That is, within both Gnosis CTF and
Branchmap, you can condition an asset on more than
one event, and the order in which you condition on the
events does not matter.
The primary distinction between Gnosis CTF tokens
andBranchmapconditioned ERC-20’s is that Gnosis
tokens utilize an opaque identifier (the address of the
oracle that resolves the event). If someone hands you a
Gnosis CTF token with no additional context, there is no
way to ascertain which event(s) and which outcome(s) it
is conditioned on. ABranchmapconditioned ERC-20,
on the other hand, has a semantic identifier: a slug for
the name of the event and outcome, which is a lookup key
for theBranchmapnamespace, which then gives you the
event, its full resolution text, the oracle which resolves it,
the event venue it was sourced from, and the addresses of
every asset, lending protocol, etc. that is also contingent
on this event.

2.2 Arrow-Debreu theory
We view multiverse finance as a pragmatic attempt at uti-
lizing existing prediction market infrastructure to extend
the space of possible financial instruments towards the
Arrow-Debreu model for complete markets [citation]. In
this section we explain how multiverse finance fits into the
model of Arrow-Debreu complete markets.
In the course of developing multiverse finance, we must
thread the needle between adding too much complexity
to the economy and not enough, and of what type. Mul-
tiverse finance provides a UX and opinionated take on
the many different “coordinate systems” one could use to
define products in the space ofArrow securities, essentially
‘claims on assets contingent on some future event’, defined
below. New types of instruments create more avenues for
risk-transfer. The more the economy grows in complexity,
in terms of number and quantity of resources and resource
transformation processes, the more we need new instru-
ments to handle new types of risk. Not having enough
instrumental complexity creates a market that is blind
to its own actions, resulting in poorer outcomes. Having
too much instrumental complexity results in a market in
which actors cannot make rational decisions, resulting in
poorer outcomes. Thus great care must be taken when
creating new instruments. However, given the dramatically
increased capacity to handle new instrumental complexity
enabled by AI, we believe that now is the time in which
bigger steps towards complete markets can be made.
We believe that existing risk-transfer mechanisms are too
constrained to handle the exponentially growing amount
of complexity in resource and resource transformations.
In simplified terms, Arrow-Debreu theory implies that
the current price of an asset is a probabilistically-weighted
average of the price of a claim on that asset in all future
states of the world.

3.3.1 Key-value store
As mentioned in subsection 1.4.2,Branchmapis a fork

Branchmapis thus organized as a hierarchical set of
paths much like a file system, and under each path some
number of mutable and immutable keys and their corre-
sponding values may be stored. These keys and values
ultimately act as an index for the event spaceE. Here
we paraphrase a relevant section of the Hypermap white
paper, asBranchmapis identical:
1. All keys are strings containing exclusively characters
0–9,a–z(lowercase), and-(hyphen).
2. A key may be one of two types: aname-keyor adata-
key.
3. Every name-key may create sub-entries directly beneath
it.
4. Every name-key is an ERC-721 NFT, with an associated
token-bound account having a counterfactual address,
implemented according to the ERC-6551 standard.
5. The implementation of a token-bound account may be
selected when a name-key is created.
6. Every name-key may create data-keys directly beneath
it.
7. A data-key may be either mutable (anote, whose label
is prefixed by~) or immutable (afact, whose label is
prefixed by!).
The root entry ofBranchmapis denoted.branch. We

4.3 Multiverse-only instruments
Branchmapallows for instruments that are not merely
existing financial instruments with a conditional wrapper.
It allows for completely new instruments native to multi-
verse finance that only make sense when future states are
first-class financial contexts.
4.3.1 Multiverse HELOC
A multiverse home-equity line of credit allows borrowing
terms to vary across future states. For example, a user
may borrow against the value of their home in a verse
where the expected value of that home has risen, and then
sell that claim to another party (likely a sophisticated one
that specializes in pricing such claims). More generally, a
person may borrow against future income, equity, or asset
values only in worlds where specified macroeconomic or
personal conditions hold. This allows for, among other
things, a generalization of the concept of a collar in options
trading. They can sell excess upside and use it to buy
downside protection.
4.3.2 Conditional smart contracts
A conditional smart contract changes its execution path
depending on event outcomes. Unlike an ordinary contract
with an oracle trigger, a conditional smart contract can
hold, route, or settle assets that already live inside the
corresponding verse.
4.3.3 Cybernetic hypothesis vault
A cybernetic hypothesis vault allocates capital across hy-
potheses, observes outcomes, and updates future alloca-
tions based on measured causal impact. Such a vault
requires named hypotheses, interventions, outcome claims,
and impact measurements.
4.3.4 Counterfactual protocol rollout
A protocol can issue assets or incentives conditional on
whether a proposed rollout, parameter change, or gover-
nance action succeeds. This allows communities to trade
andfundcounterfactualprotocolfuturesbeforecommitting
to them.
4.3.5 Startup funding
Startup financing can become conditional on milestones,
market states, governance events, product launches, or
ecosystem adoption. Multiverse-native instruments can
encode upside, downside, and contingent support more
precisely than conventional SAFEs, notes, or equity.
4.4 Relationship to Futarchy
Futarchy proposes governance by betting on outcomes
conditional on policy decisions. Multiverse finance gen-
eralizes this idea. Instead of only asking which policy
maximizes a scalar welfare metric, it allows entire financial
systems to be instantiated, traded, and compared across
policy-conditioned worlds.

the allocation and value of assets within each branch.

2. predicate identifiers;
3. settlement specifications;
4. oracle references;

7. Application integration
One of the main ideas behindBranchmapis to make it
easy for existing protocols to integrate multiverse finance.
Here we discuss what this may look like.
7.1 DEX integration
A DEX can integrate withBranchmapby recognizing
Branch Cash wrappers, reading verse metadata, and en-
forcing same-verse composition rules where appropriate.
The DEX need not adopt any particular AMM design.
7.2 Prediction market integration
A prediction market can integrate by claiming a venue sub-
space and publishing signed market manifests. This allows
its markets to become discoverable, comparable, and com-
posable across the broader multiverse finance ecosystem.
7.3 Lending protocol integration
A lending protocol can useBranchmapto determine
which conditional assets inhabit compatible verses. This
enables lending markets where collateral and debt share
the same conditional state.
7.4 Structured products
Structured products can be represented as portfolios over
verses. A product may pay differently across election
outcomes, macroeconomic regimes, governance decisions,
protocol launches, or personal milestones.
7.5 AI Agents
AI agents require machine-readable market structure, event
identity, settlement rules, and cross-venue relationships.
Branchmapprovides the semantic substrate for agents
to discover, compare, and compose multiverse financial
opportunities.

9. Conclusion
Branchmapis a namespace for the financial multiverse
that gives a concrete implementation path towards Arrow-
Debreu complete markets. Conditional-token primitives
make it possible to create assets whose payoffs depend on
future outcomes, but they do not by themselves create
a navigable, interoperable, or institutionally credible fi-
nancial system.Branchmapsupplies the missing map:
names, relationships, provenance, ownership, and integra-
tion surfaces for events, claims, venues, conditional assets,
and in-universe protocols.
The initialEVMpilot aims to demonstrate this archi-
tecture on a single chain. The long-term goal is a decen-
tralized cross-chain namespace where prediction markets,
DEXes, lending protocols, structured products, AI agents,
and event venues can coordinate around shared future-
state identities without collapsing important settlement
distinctions.
